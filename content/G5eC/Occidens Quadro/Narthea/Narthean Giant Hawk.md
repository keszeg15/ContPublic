### Keen Sight

The hawk has advantage on Wisdom (Perception) checks that rely on sight.

### Flyby

The hawk doesn't provoke opportunity attacks when it flies out of an enemy's reach.

### Aerial Predator

If the hawk moves at least 20 feet straight toward a creature and then hits it with a Talons attack on the same turn, the target must succeed on a DC 13 Strength saving throw or be grappled (escape DC 13). While grappling a creature, the hawk's flying speed is reduced to 60 feet.

### Drop Hunter

While flying, the hawk can release a grappled creature at any point during its movement (no action required).

## Actions

### Multiattack

The hawk makes two attacks: one with its Beak and one with its Talons.

### Beak

_Melee Weapon Attack:_ +5 to hit, reach 5 ft., one target.  
_Hit:_ 7 (1d8 + 3) piercing damage.

### Talons

_Melee Weapon Attack:_ +5 to hit, reach 5 ft., one target.  
_Hit:_ 10 (2d6 + 3) slashing damage.

If the target is Medium or smaller, the hawk may immediately attempt to grapple it (escape DC 13).

### Death Drop (Recharge 5–6)

The hawk releases a creature it is grappling while at least 30 feet above the ground. The creature falls normally and takes falling damage. If the creature survives the fall and lands prone, the hawk may move up to half its flying speed toward it without provoking opportunity attacks.

## Tactics

A Narthean Giant Hawk rarely fights toe-to-toe. It circles high overhead, selecting isolated prey. It swoops down, strikes with its talons, and attempts to carry off Medium or smaller creatures. Once it reaches a sufficient height—typically 50 to 100 feet—it releases the victim and dives after them to finish the kill.

Pairs sometimes hunt together, with one bird distracting prey while the other attempts a grab. They commonly prey on goats, deer, giant rabbits, and unwary travelers crossing open terrain.
